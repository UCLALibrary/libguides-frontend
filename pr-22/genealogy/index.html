General Notes

The UCLA Library has many resources which could potentially be used for genealogy research, but our resources are not comprehensive and are not organized to support genealogy research. In addition, UCLA Library staff must give priority to assisting current UCLA students, faculty, and staff, so we may not have the staff resources to assist genealogy researchers with in-depth questions.

For these reasons, this guide is meant to help you identify other resources available to genealogy researchers in Southern California.

Where to Begin Your Genealogy Research in Southern California

Because UCLA Library does not specialize in supporting genealogical research, we recommend starting your research at one of these institutions, in person or online.

In Person

  • Los Angeles FamilySearch Center (Westwood LDS Temple) This link opens in a new window
    Located near UCLA at 10741 Santa Monica Boulevard in Los Angeles.
  • Los Angeles Public Library — History and Genealogy Department This link opens in a new window
    Located on Lower Level Four of the Tom Bradley Wing at the Central Library in downtown Los Angeles.
  • Southern California Genealogical Society and Family Research Library This link opens in a new window
    Located at 417 Irving Drive in Burbank, CA.

Online

  • Southern California Genealogical Society and Family Research Library This link opens in a new window

Genealogy Resources in the UCLA Library

This is a selection of genealogical resources available at the UCLA Library. For more resources, try a subject search in the Library Catalog for genealogy and your desired geographical region (United States, England) or group (Chinese, Jews).

Please note: If the resource has a blue key, it is only available to non-UCLA users (including UCLA alumni, emeriti, and retirees) while physically on campus.

  • Ancestry Library Edition This link opens in a new window
    • This link opens in a new window

    An extensive collection of family history research databases, including the US federal census images from 1790 to 1950. Please note there are differences in features and content between Ancestry Library Edition and Ancestry.com subscriptions for individuals. 

  • Bureau of Land Management — General Land Office Records This link opens in a new window
    Access to Federal land conveyance records for the Public Land States, including image access to more than five million Federal land title records issued between 1820 and the present. Also images related to survey plats and field notes, dating back to 1810.
  • Castle Garden This link opens in a new window
    An educational project of The Battery Conservancy. This database contains information on 11 million immigrants from 1820 through 1892, the year Ellis Island opened. The records are now part of Ancestry.com
  • Oral History Online This link opens in a new window
    • This link opens in a new window
    Indexes more than 2,700 collections of oral history in English from around the world. Provides keyword searching of full-text transcripts when available, plus links to audio and video files.
  • Passenger and Immigration Lists Bibliography, 1538-1900 This link opens in a new window
    A print bibliography by P. William Filby (1988).
  • ProQuest Newspapers This link opens in a new window
    • This link opens in a new window

    Searches all ProQuest newspapers to which UCLA subscribes, both current and historical newspapers from major U.S. cities (Los Angeles Times, New York Times, Wall Street Journal), international news sources, and alternative press.

  • UCLA Center for Oral History Research This link opens in a new window
    The UCLA Center for Oral History Research (COHR) conducts in-depth, multi-session oral history interviews with individuals who have been a part of the history of Los Angeles and its many communities. COHR has particularly strong collections in the history of social movements, communities of color, the arts, Los Angeles politics and government, and the history of UCLA.

Biographical Information

History scholars often need reference materials related to biographical information and this information can sometimes be difficult to track down. Here are some other places to start when looking for these materials. Keep in mind that these resources are not comprehensive and feature prominent individuals, so you may not find a lot of your ancestors unless they were significantly influential in their profession, in their government, in high profile historical events, etc. Don't forget to try newspapers that are contemporary to the individual in question.

Please note: If the resource has a blue key, it is only available to non-UCLA users (including UCLA alumni, emeriti, and retirees) while physically on campus.

  • LAPL Historic City and Business & Phone Directories This link opens in a new window

    Contains biographical profiles of people from all centuries as well as all countries and regions worldwide. Compiles biographical articles from printed reference works published from the 16th to the 20th century, and reproduces these original documents as facsimile images on the screen.

Some Free Resources on the Web

  • FamilySearch.org This link opens in a new window
    FamilySearch is the website of the genealogical arm of the LDS Church. They have digitized and indexed a large amount of primary source material from across the US and around the world, which is available for free online.
  • US GenWeb Project This link opens in a new window
    US GenWeb provides links to and information about genealogical resources at the state and county levels for the entire US. There is also a World GenWeb which treats every country in the world.
  • Vitalrec.com This link opens in a new window
    This is one of many free sites that will give you information and addresses on where to find vital records for each US county.

Some Inspiration

Sometimes genealogical research can be frustrating, when you feel that you've hit a brick wall and don't know where to turn next. This selection of TV programs and non-fiction narratives about genealogy might help you feel inspired again—and maybe will give you some research ideas.

  • Finding Your Roots with Henry Louis Gates, Jr. (PBS) This link opens in a new window
    Finding Your Roots is similar to Who Do You Think You Are? in that it focuses on the ancestries of the famous but has a slightly more scholarly tone. It also includes more DNA research and work with ethnic/religious minorities.
  • Slaves in the Family - Edward Ball (published 1998) This link opens in a new window
    A non-fiction account of Edward Ball's research into the lives of both his slave-holding ancestors and their slaves.
  • Roots - Alex Haley (published 1976) This link opens in a new window
    This ground-breaking work doesn't require much introduction.
  • The Lost : a Search for Six of Six Million - Daniel Mendelsohn (published 2006) This link opens in a new window
    Daniel Mendelsohn's search for six members of his family who perished in the Holocaust.
