Open Access Digital Resources

  • Directed by Women This link opens in a new window
    • This link opens in a new window
    Tumblr account dedicated to celebrating women directors, with information about their work and impact.
  • Edited By This link opens in a new window
    • This link opens in a new window
    An historical survey of female film editors who contributed to the art of film editing, compiled by scholar Su Friedrich.
  • Films for the Feminist Classroom This link opens in a new window
    • This link opens in a new window
    Open-access journal publishing film reviews that explore the value of films as pedagogical tools in the feminist classroom. Special features, such as interviews with filmmakers, reviews of film festivals, and discussions about pedagogy, further promote and support our aims to be a resource for educators, librarians, and others in educational spaces.
  • The Interviews: An Oral History of Television This link opens in a new window
    • This link opens in a new window
    From the Television Academy Foundation, a collection of over 900 oral history interviews with TV professionals across a range of specialties and genres.
  • Leader Ladies Project This link opens in a new window
    • This link opens in a new window
    The Leader Ladies Project presents a collection of “China Girls” (or “Leader Ladies") – the test images of (most often) women that sometimes appear in the countdown that begins every reel of motion picture film meant for exhibition.
  • Love, Links, Archives: The Wendy Clarke Collection This link opens in a new window
    • This link opens in a new window
    Digital project from The Wisconsin Center for Film and Theater Research. Since the early-1970s, Wendy Clarke has expanded the possibilities of using video as a medium for artistic expression and human connection. Encompassing hundreds of hours of footage, from across over a dozen video formats, the Wendy Clarke collection represents a unique audiovisual archive of American life.
  • Media History Digital Library This link opens in a new window
    • This link opens in a new window
    Freely available online portal, featuring millions of pages of books and magazines from the histories of film, broadcasting, and recorded sound.
  • The Not So Silver Screen: Black Women in Media This link opens in a new window
    • This link opens in a new window
    Professor Kimberlé Crenshaw moderates a 2017 panel at the Hammer Museum that explores the invisibility and devaluing of black women in media culture as performers, producers, and directors, alongside solutions to promote black women as creators. Panelists: legendary actress Diahann Carroll; stage and soap actress Tonya Pinkins; film, television, and theater actress and director LisaGay Hamilton; veteran Hollywood casting director Tracy "Twinkie" Byrd; April Reign, #OscarsSoWhite creator and the founder and editor of BroadwayBlack.com; University of Alabama professor Kristen Warner, who studies race, representation, and the media; and writer and director Gina Prince-Bythewood.
  • UCLA Center for Oral History Research (COHR) This link opens in a new window
    • This link opens in a new window
    COHR conducts in-depth, multi-session oral history interviews with individuals who have been a part of the history of Los Angeles and its many communities. Over 2000 interviews document a wide range of areas related to Southern California history, including the histories of Black, indigenous, people of color and immigrant communities, the arts, political activism, politics and government, and UCLA history. Transcripts available in Library Special Collections (YRL).
  • Women Film Pioneers Project This link opens in a new window
    • This link opens in a new window
    Features information on silent-era producers, directors, co-directors, scenario writers, scenario editors, camera operators, title writers, editors, costume designers, and exhibitors. From Columbia University.
  • Women in Visual Effects This link opens in a new window
    • This link opens in a new window
    A portrait and video series aimed to highlight and advocate the talented women within the visual effects field of film and television.

Data and Reports on Women in the Film and Television Industry

  • Celluloid Ceiling Report This link opens in a new window
    Annual report led by Dr. Martha M. Lauzen that studies female representation in front of and behind the camera for top-grossing Hollywood films.
  • Center for Scholars and Storytellers Reports (UCLA) This link opens in a new window
    Includes several reports on film, television, and theater entertainment, including "Beyond Checking a Box: A Lack of Authentically Inclusive Representation Has Costs at the Box Office."
  • Geena Davis Institute on Gender in Media: Research Reports This link opens in a new window
    The Institute compiles reports on industry topics related to gender disparity both on screen and behind the camera.
  • Hollywood Diversity Report (UCLA Bunche Center) This link opens in a new window
    A yearly report (2014-present) on diversity in the film and television industry.
  • Hollywood Writers Report This link opens in a new window
    From the Writers' Guild of America, this report "provides new information on the progress of women, minority, and older writers on the television employment front."
  • Inequality in 1,100 Popular Films: Examining Portrayals of Gender, Race/Ethnicity, LGBT, and Disability from 2007-2017 This link opens in a new window
    A 2018 study from USC Annenberg that examines representation on screen and behind the camera.
  • Women's Media Center Reports This link opens in a new window
    Compiles data and offers analysis on women's representation in media, including Oscar and Emmy nominations, news media, and overall status in the media landscape.

Connect from Off-Campus

The Library's online subscription resources can always be accessed from campus computers and wireless networks. However, off-campus access is restricted to current UCLA students, faculty and staff who have set up their computer using one of the methods below.

  • UCLA Virtual Private Network (VPN) This link opens in a new window
    A program you install on your computer. Already built-in on most mobile devices.
  • UCLA Online Proxy Server This link opens in a new window
    A simple browser setting which will automatically divert you to a UCLA logon page when you first access a restricted site.

Please note, if you are using a Mac, Safari may not work to read some of the PDF material; try Chrome or Firefox.

If you need further assistance, you can contact UCLA Digital & Technology Solutions IT Support at (310) 267-4357 (24/7) or help@it.ucla.edu.
